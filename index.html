About Us

Let’s Connect — in spaces not built for us

Tukonnect is a blend of Swahili and English. In Swahili, “Tuko” means “we are.” Combined with the English word “Connect”, the name means “Let’s Connect.”

It is a call to LGBTQ folks to come together, find each other, and build something in digital spaces that were not built for them.

Tukonnect Digital Hub is a Kenya-based organization working at the intersection of digital inclusion, safety, and rights-based advocacy. Our work is built around LGBTQ folks — communities most targeted by digital harm, most excluded from digital opportunity, and most absent from the spaces where digital policy is shaped.

We are based in Kisumu, rooted in East Africa, and oriented toward a digital future that is safe and equitable for everyone, regardless of gender identity, sexual orientation, or background.

Our team brings together civil society practice, programme management, community organizing, and digital rights expertise. We are a member of Women in AI Kenya, and our leadership reflects the communities we serve.

Our Direction

Mission & Vision

We are building digital futures rooted in safety, inclusion, rights, and community power.

M

Mission

To advance the digital inclusion, safety, and power of LGBTQ people and women in East Africa, so they can fully participate in, shape, and benefit from the digital and AI-driven world.

V

Vision

A digital East Africa where LGBTQ people and women are safe online, literate in emerging technologies, represented in governance spaces, and equipped to drive the digital future on their own terms.

The Problem We Are Solving

Three gaps sit at the core of our work

LGBTQ folks face compounding barriers in digital spaces. They are disproportionately targeted by online harassment, surveillance, doxxing, and hate, while being excluded from skills, resources, and policy spaces.

GAP 01

Safety & Rights Gap

LGBTQ folks are primary targets of digital violence, yet digital safety education rarely reaches them in culturally grounded, accessible formats.

GAP 02

Literacy & Access Gap

AI and digital skills programmes are often built for already-privileged audiences. LGBTQ communities in grassroots settings are consistently left out.

GAP 03

Governance Gap

Policies shaping AI, data, and platform regulation are being written without the voices of those most affected. LGBTQ folks are largely absent from these processes in Kenya and across the region.

What Guides Us

Our Values

Every programme, partnership, and public communication is shaped by our responsibility to community safety and justice.

01

Safety First

The safety of LGBTQ folks, online and offline, is non-negotiable. Every programme, partnership, and public communication is evaluated through a safety lens.

02

Inclusion

We center those most marginalized within already marginalized communities: queer women, trans people, women with disabilities, and those in informal settlements and rural areas.

03

Community Power

We build from the ground up. The expertise of LGBTQ people and women who have navigated these systems is the foundation of everything we do.

04

Justice

We see digital rights as inseparable from LGBTQ rights. Technology is never neutral. We name that, and we act on it.

Why It Matters

At the center, not the margins

LGBTQ people and women are not on the margins of the digital revolution. They are at the center of it: as targets of digital harm, as excluded populations, and as communities with the most to gain when digital spaces are made safe and equitable.

Kenya’s AI Bill, data protection frameworks, and platform policies are being finalized now. These decisions will shape digital life for decades.

The gap we are filling

Tukonnect exists to make sure LGBTQ people and women have a voice in those decisions, and the tools to protect themselves in the meantime.

Very few organizations do this work at the community level in East Africa with an explicit LGBTQ and gender lens.
